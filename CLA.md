# Contributor License Agreement

**Draft — have a lawyer read this before you rely on it.** It is modelled on the
shape of the Apache Individual CLA, written to be short enough that a contributor
will actually read it. It has not been reviewed by anyone qualified to review it.

---

## Why this exists

Claude Mate is open to contributions. This document exists so that being open to
contributions does not quietly cost the author the ability to decide what happens
to the project.

Without an agreement like this one, **every contributor owns the copyright in
their own patch.** Nothing about accepting a pull request changes that. The
practical consequence is not obvious until you hit it: the project can only ever
be distributed under the licence the contributions arrived under. Changing that
licence later — to publish the game commercially, to ship it in a product, to
move to a different open-source licence, or simply to fix a licence that turned
out to be a bad fit — needs the written permission of **every contributor who
still has code in the tree**, including the ones who have changed email address,
left the industry, or died. Projects have been permanently frozen by this.

So this agreement asks contributors for a licence broad enough that the project
stays steerable, while leaving them owning their own work.

documentation) to this project, you agree to the following.

display, publicly perform, and distribute your contribution and such derivative

That last clause is the whole point of the document. It is what allows the
project to change licence, or to be published commercially, without a hunt for
every past contributor.

**4. You confirm you are allowed to give it.** You represent that each

**5. If your employer owns your work,** which is common, you confirm you have
permission to contribute, or that your employer has waived its rights in the

**6. Nothing is guaranteed to be used.** Accepting this agreement does not oblige
the project to merge, keep, or ship anything you submit.

## How to accept

State in your first pull request:

> I have read `CLA.md` and I agree to it.

One statement covers everything you contribute afterwards. If you would rather
not agree, please open an issue describing the change instead of a pull request —
ideas discussed in an issue are not contributions under this document, and you
are welcome to build your own version of anything discussed there.

## What this does not cover

**It does not make the game idea private.** Nothing in this agreement, and
nothing in any licence, prevents someone from reading the design and building
their own version. Game *rules and mechanics are not protectable by copyright* —
they are systems, and systems are excluded. "A platformer where the terrain is a
contribution graph and the deadline is your health" is an idea, and ideas are
free. What is protected is the **expression**: this source, this art, this level
data, this prose, and the **name**, which is a trademark question rather than a
copyright one.

That distinction is worth understanding rather than working around. Enforcing it
is done by keeping the copyrightable material clearly attributed and licensed —
which is what this document and [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) do between them.
